About me

I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Cybersecurity at the Rochester Institute of Technology. Before joining RIT, I received my Ph.D. in Computer Science from the University of Delaware in 2021, supervised by Dr. Rui Zhang. I received my B.E. and M.E. degrees, both in Computer Science, from Hangzhou Dianzi University, China.

My research is on cybersecurity and data privacy, with a current focus on local differential privacy, privacy-preserving AI, and trustworthy agentic AI. Earlier work covers privacy in mobile sensing and crowdsourcing, integrity of outsourced computation, and the security of wireless sensing and localization. My work has appeared at ICNP, INFOCOM, TDSC, TON, and PETs.

I am a recipient of the NSF CRII Award (2023) and a Meta Research Award (2022), and our papers have received the Andreas Pfitzmann Best Student Paper Award at PETs (2026), an Artifact Award Runner-up at PETs (2025), the Best Paper Award at IEEE CNS (2023), and a Best Paper Runner-up Award at IWQoS (2019).

I am looking for highly self-motivated Ph.D. students to work on data privacy, privacy-preserving machine learning, and data protection in networked and distributed systems. If you are interested, please email me.

Teaching

  • CSEC-721 · 3 credits Advanced Data Privacy
  • CSEC-472 · 3 credits Authentication and Security Models

    The theory, design, and implementation of the systems behind identification, authentication, authorization, and accountability, with a focus on trust at each layer. Students examine formal models of access control, approaches to system accreditation, the application of cryptography to authentication, and how IAAA principles are implemented in modern operating systems.

  • CSEC-759 · 3 credits Special Topics in Computing Security

    A rotating course on current topics in computing security, with the focal area set each time it is offered.
